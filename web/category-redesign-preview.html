One catalog-overview rail — breadth + earnings

  • It's still a category overview. The rail is the whole catalog ("1,247 items"), browsable, with "See all" → full grid + sort/filter (All / Most valuable / Newest / By set). Users see what exists, not just a top-4.
  • Default sort = Most valuable because commission is a % of price (a €420 Charizard pays ~30–80× a €5 common) — breadth is preserved, but the highest-earning items lead.
  • The museum is where money is made (the affiliate "Where to buy" rail). The overview's job is to funnel taps into it. One focused rail > six competing ones.
  • High-value categories route to high-rate networks (Catawiki ~7–10%, Master of Malt) where a match exists.

🏷️ Brands as a marketing channel (the future model)

A brand "claims" its category and runs it like an Amazon Brand Store — except it sits on top of a page full of real owners, valuations, and buyers. The pitch: a warm audience of collectors already browsing your category.

What a brand can add (purple = paid):

  • Official banner + verified badge — claim the header, brand colors/logo, "Follow" to build an owned audience.
  • Sponsored "Featured by [Brand]" slot — promote new drops, pre-orders, restocks above the organic catalog.
  • Official store CTA — a direct link to the brand's own storefront (they keep 100%; you charge for placement).
  • Boosted catalog items — pin/highlight specific SKUs in the overview rail.
  • Brand announcements & events — set launches, signings, conventions surfaced to followers.

Pricing tiers (suggested):

Sponsored slot — €/mo placement Verified banner + Follow — €€/mo Full category takeover — €€€/mo

Why it works: you already own the audience, the data (what's valuable, what's trending, who collects it), and the surface. Brands pay for access to intent — collectors deciding what to buy next — which is exactly what the museum → "Where to buy" flow captures. Affiliate is the default revenue; brand sponsorship is the premium layer on the same page.
